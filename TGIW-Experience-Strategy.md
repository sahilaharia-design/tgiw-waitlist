# TGIW WAITLIST — CREATIVE CONCEPT & EXPERIENCE STRATEGY
### Prepared by: The Launch Team
### Classification: Internal Creative Direction

---

> *"The goal is not to make someone want to buy something.  
> The goal is to make someone feel they've already been there —  
> and can't imagine having missed it."*

---

## THE SINGLE IDEA

**You don't visit the website. You attend the wedding.**

The current version treats the illustration as decoration. The illustration IS the world. It doesn't live behind the content — it IS the content. Every character, every corner, every candle flame is a story waiting to be found.

The design challenge isn't "how do we make a great landing page."

It's: **how do we make a 2-minute visit feel like a 3-hour memory.**

---

# SECTION 1: EXPERIENCE PHILOSOPHY

## What Should Visitors Feel?

**On arrival:** *Intrigue bordering on confusion. In the best possible way.* They shouldn't immediately understand what this is. They should feel they've stepped into something that is already happening — the way you feel walking into a wedding where you don't know anyone yet but the energy pulls you in.

**During exploration:** *Belonging.* The discovery should feel earned. Not "I found the CTA" — but "I found the cousin who's already crying before the ceremony." The illustration rewards the curious.

**At RSVP:** *Like accepting an honour.* Not filling a form. Being asked to be there.

**After signup:** *Ownership.* They are now part of this story. Their name is in the world. The wedding doesn't happen without them.

## What Should They Believe?

That this is unlike anything they've experienced in an Indian cultural product.

That whoever made this understands the wedding in a way that no brand, film, or game ever has — the *emotional truth* of it. Not the spectacle. The feeling.

That if the *waitlist* is this good, the thing itself must be extraordinary.

## What Should They Wonder?

- *Who are all these characters?*
- *What are those five glowing zones I can see in the corner?*
- *Why did that character just look at me?*
- *What happens if I click the DJ?*
- *How do I become Family tier?*
- *Was that sound real?*

## What Should They Remember?

One specific moment. Not the whole page — one moment.

The character who whispered something. The envelope appearing. The seat number with their name on it. The family tree with their branch growing.

That's the moment they share. That's the moment that makes this Awwwards.

# SECTION 2: THE IDEAL USER JOURNEY

## 0–5 Seconds: The Summons

**Black screen.** Absolute darkness. Silence.

Then: the faintest sound of a shehnai, impossibly far away.

Then: a single flame. A diya. Just the flame.

Then: gold letterpress text fades in from the dark — not a headline, not a brand name. A formal invitation in both English and Devanagari:

> *"आपको सादर आमंत्रित किया गया है।"*  
> *"You have been cordially summoned."*

No logo. No nav. No CTA. Just the summons.

**Emotional target:** "What is this?"

## 5–30 Seconds: The World Stirs

As the user hovers or moves, the flame responds. The diya tracks the cursor, subtly.

Behind it: the illustration begins breathing into existence. Not all at once — regions of it emerge from the dark as the flame moves nearby. Gold light spills outward, revealing detail. A palace parapet. A marigold garland. A figure in the distance dancing. Then dark again.

The illustration is not a background image. It is a living atlas. And right now it is half-asleep.

**Emotional target:** "I want to see more."

## 30–90 Seconds: The Invitation

An envelope materializes from the dark. Gold. Wax-sealed. It bears the user's cursor position as if it knows them.

The seal: not a CTA, not a button. A hand-pressed emboss that invites a click.

When clicked: the envelope opens. Inside: a gilded card. Handwritten script. Embossed. **The five days** listed as items on a formal wedding programme — not explained, just named. *Welcome Dinner. Mehndi. Sangeet. Haldi. Wedding.* Day 5: the description is blank. A smudge of ink where the text should be.

Curiosity: maximum.

**Emotional target:** "I need to know what Day 5 is."

## 90–180 Seconds: The World Opens

The card fades. The world illuminates fully — but not as a static image.

The illustration becomes interactive. Characters breathe. The DJ in the Sangeet zone shuffles a record. The photographer in the corner raises her camera — and when she does, a *flash* briefly illuminates a hidden corner of the illustration the user hadn't seen yet.

Navigation is not a nav bar. Navigation is the world itself. Move toward the Mehndi zone: the colours shift warm. Hover a character: a whispered annotation appears. *"Has been crying since the Welcome Dinner."* *"This is his fourth plate of biryani."* *"Absolutely certain this whole thing is her idea."*

**The five zones** glow when discovered — not flagged, not labelled. Just subtly alive.

**Emotional target:** "I could spend an hour here."

## 180+ Seconds: The Ask

Only after meaningful exploration does the RSVP surface.

Not as a section you scroll to. As an event. The wedding pandit — a small character in the illustration — turns toward the viewer. An invisible threshold crossed. He raises a hand, beckoning. Click him.

A new envelope. This one addressed to: *"[Your name here]."*

The RSVP is not a form. It's completing the invitation. Name. Email. Tier. Done. The seal is pressed.

**Emotional target:** "I've been invited. Of course I'm saying yes."

## Post-Signup

Fireworks — but the illustration's fireworks, not a generic burst. The scene you've been exploring lights up in celebration. Your seat is placed on a visible seating chart that shows (approximately) where all the other guests are sitting. Your name appears in a corner of the illustration. Small. Real.

The shareable artifact is not a static image. It is a personalized envelope addressed to them, from you.

## Post-Share

When their referral joins: the family tree in the illustration grows a branch with their name. The user gets a push (or email) notification: *"Your cousin just arrived."*

## Post-Email

The confirmation email is formatted as a formal Indian wedding invitation. Printed card aesthetic. Gold letterpress on deep red. Their seat number. Their tier. An *"add to calendar"* that reads as a wedding program entry.

# SECTION 3: NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE — THE FIVE ACTS

## Act 1: The Dark Before the Wedding
**Duration:** 0–8 seconds  
**Emotion:** Mystery. Reverence. The held breath before something begins.  
**Narrative:** The world is asleep. One flame exists.  
**Interaction:** Cursor movement — the flame follows, reveals.  
**Visual system:** Pure black. One warm gold point light. No UI elements visible.  
**Conversion purpose:** None. Earn attention first.

---

## Act 2: The Invitation
**Duration:** 8–45 seconds  
**Emotion:** Anticipation. The feeling of receiving something personal.  
**Narrative:** The invitation finds you. You didn't search for it.  
**Interaction:** Envelope materializes → hover animates the wax seal → click opens it.  
**Visual system:** Deep night. Warm candlelight. Gold foil. Embossed paper texture. Devanagari letterpress.  
**Conversion purpose:** Install desire. Make the user feel chosen, not marketed to.

---

## Act 3: The World
**Duration:** 45 seconds–3 minutes  
**Emotion:** Wonder. Discovery. Play. The specific joy of finding something no one told you was there.  
**Narrative:** The wedding world comes alive. The illustration reveals itself as a place with history.  
**Interaction:** Pan and explore. Hover characters. Discover zones. Find hidden stories.  
**Visual system:** The full illustration — but managed. Not everything visible at once. Light directs attention. Depth of field creates focus.  
**Conversion purpose:** Dwell time. Emotional investment. The longer they stay, the more they want it.

---

## Act 4: The Ask
**Duration:** Variable — triggered by exploration depth, not scroll position  
**Emotion:** Honour. The RSVP feels like a privilege, not a transaction.  
**Narrative:** The wedding pandit beckons. You've been here long enough to be seen.  
**Interaction:** RSVP as invitation acceptance. Name and email as writing your name on the guest list.  
**Visual system:** Close focus on the invitation artifact. World softly dims around it. Intimate.  
**Conversion purpose:** The conversion. But it doesn't feel like one.

---

## Act 5: You Belong
**Duration:** Post-signup, persistent  
**Emotion:** Pride. Belonging. The warmth of being included in something special.  
**Narrative:** You are now a character in this world. The wedding has your name in it.  
**Interaction:** Shareable invitation card. Visible seat on the seating chart. Family tree if referrals made.  
**Visual system:** Celebration. The illustration at full luminosity. Fireworks.  
**Conversion purpose:** Viral loop. Every share is a new invitation arriving in someone else's dark.

# SECTION 4: ART DIRECTION

## Typography

**Display (Invitations, headers):** A bespoke serif with ink-press imperfection. References: Freight Display, or a custom letterpress cut. Pairs with hand-lettered Devanagari for bilingual moments.

**Body (Whispers, annotations):** Cormorant Garamond Italic. Gossip-weight. The font of secrets told at weddings.

**Labels, numbers, UI:** Space Mono. The lone modernity in an otherwise handcrafted world. Used sparingly — it represents the "real world" punctuating the fantasy.

**Never use:** Anything that reads as "tech." No Inter. No DM Sans. No system fonts in hero positions.

## Colour System

The wrong instinct: saffron, fuchsia, Bollywood.

The right instinct: **palace at 2am.**

- **Background darkness:** `#0d0b08` — not pure black. Warm, like a room lit only by candles.
- **Primary gold:** `#c9930a` — aged, not bright. Museum gold, not party gold.
- **Deep red:** `#7a1c18` — not the red of festival, the red of velvet curtains.
- **Teal shadow:** `#0d3d3c` — the colour of peacock feathers in darkness.
- **Paper:** `#f0e8d5` — aged invitation card. Warm cream with texture.
- **Light:** `#ffd97a` — the diya flame. The single source of everything.

The illustration's own colours ARE the colour palette. The UI should never compete with them — it should feel like it was printed on the same card.

## Texture System

Three texture layers:

1. **Paper grain** — subtle noise over all backgrounds. Not visible, but felt.
2. **Fabric** — the illustration edges bleed into the UI frame as if the world is embroidered into the page.
3. **Light bleed** — warm flare where the illustration meets dark areas. No hard edges anywhere.

## Lighting System

**One source. Always.**

The diya. Everything in the experience radiates from that single warm point. Characters in the illustration who are far from the current light source are in shadow. Moving the light (via scroll or cursor) reveals them.

This is not just an aesthetic choice. It is the *interaction model.* Light = attention. You control the light. You choose what gets revealed.

## Illustration Treatment

Never use the illustration as a background image.

Use it as: **a living environment.**

Technical requirements:
- SVG or high-resolution layered PNG with character-level depth compositing
- Characters as individual interactive elements at hover/click threshold
- Parallax layers: foreground figures, mid-ground scene, background architecture, sky
- Ambient character animations: breathing, subtle movement, blinking — at 0.05–0.1 opacity change, so it registers subconsciously
- Zone-based colour temperature shifts controlled by scroll position

## Composition Rules

1. **Never center-justify everything.** Luxury editorial composition is asymmetric.
2. **Negative space is not wasted space.** The dark areas create tension that makes the lit areas feel earned.
3. **Type never competes with illustration.** If the illustration is active, type is ghost-light. If type is reading, illustration recedes.
4. **Every cut is a scene cut.** Transitions between modes should feel like a film edit, not a page reload.

## Editorial & Cinematic Influences

- **Editorial:** AnOther Magazine, Kinfolk, Vogue India (not the ads — the fashion editorials)
- **Cinematic:** *Maqbool* (2003) lighting. *The Favourite* (2018) compositions. *Mughal-E-Azam* (1960) negative space.
- **Invitation design:** Piaget and Cartier invitation suites. Devi Kroell packaging. Sabyasachi editorial.
- **Interactive:** Immersive Van Gogh / teamLab — but contained, not overwhelming.

# SECTION 5: MOTION DIRECTION

## The Single Motion Principle

**Everything that moves, moves because light touched it.**

No gratuitous animation. Motion is causally linked to the light source (cursor, scroll position, interaction depth). If the user hasn't "shone light" on something, it is still.

This makes motion feel magical rather than designed.

## Scroll Choreography

Abandon linear scroll-as-time.

Instead: two modes.

**Mode 1 — Approach (vertical scroll):** Used only for the pre-world sections (dark → invitation). Classical scroll. Slow, ceremonial.

**Mode 2 — Exploration (2D pan):** Once the world opens, scroll becomes pan. The page width expands to contain the full illustration. Scroll horizontally. Zoom in with pinch/wheel. The world is a map you navigate.

This is the paradigm shift. The moment the world opens, the rules of the page change. Visitors feel it immediately.

## Parallax

Four depth layers with distinct speeds:
1. **Sky/fireworks:** 0.1× scroll speed — nearly fixed
2. **Architecture/palace:** 0.25× — slow drift  
3. **Mid-scene characters:** 0.5× — natural movement
4. **Foreground figures/objects:** 0.85× — close, fast

The depth creates the sensation of *being inside the scene* rather than looking at it.

## Camera Movement

On the invitation close-up: a *very* slow push-in. 0.3px/frame. Imperceptible in isolation. Creates presence.

On zone discovery: the camera subtly re-frames — not a cut, a lean. As if looking more carefully.

On RSVP trigger: the world softly defocuses (CSS blur: 2px over 800ms) as the invitation card sharpens. Focus pull. Cinema.

## Environmental Animation

Characters in the illustration should never be fully static. At very low amplitude:

- Fabric/clothing shifts: slow sine wave, 4–6 second period
- Flame elements: organic flicker using Perlin noise
- Flower petals: occasional drift at 0.3 opacity
- The DJ's record: perpetual slow rotation
- Fairy lights: slow pulse at 3-second intervals

None of these animations should be individually noticeable. Together, they make the world feel *alive.*

## Micro-interactions

| Trigger | Response |
|---|---|
| Hover a character | Name/description whisper fades in at 11px italic |
| Hover a zone | Zone colour temperature warms; faint zone music rises |
| Click a character | Character "acknowledges" — a subtle turn toward you, a gesture |
| Hover wax seal | Seal breathes — scale 1.0 → 1.03 over 600ms |
| Form field focus | Surrounding world softly dims; field is the only lit thing |
| Submit RSVP | Envelope seal animation; world celebration bursts |

## Motion References

- **Invitation opening:** Wren Agency invitation film. Ceremonious.
- **World reveal:** teamLab's *Flowers* — organic emergence from dark.
- **Character behaviour:** *Spirited Away* backgrounds — background characters have full internal lives.
- **Parallax depth:** The Boat interactive. *À la folie* by FIELD.io.
- **Text reveals:** A24 title design — the way a film title lands.

## Animation Timing

- **Ceremonial actions** (envelope open, world reveal): 1200–2400ms. Never rushed.
- **Discovery responses** (hover characters): 240–400ms. Responsive, not instantaneous.
- **Micro-feedback** (field focus, button hover): 120–200ms. Snappy.
- **Transitions between modes**: 800–1200ms with custom ease.

Custom easing for all primary animations: `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — a slow start, fast middle, soft landing. The easing of something placed carefully, not dropped.

# SECTION 6: ILLUSTRATION UTILIZATION

## The Paradigm Shift

The illustration is not being used. It is being wasted.

The correct framing: **the illustration is a 100-room palace. The current version opens the front door, takes one photo, and sends you home.**

## How to Travel Through It

**Technical approach:** PixiJS or Three.js scene. The illustration is treated as a composited world with:
- Individual character sprites at interactive layer
- Background as zoomable texture (4K+ source, lazy-loaded by region)
- Viewport as a "camera" that the user moves through

On desktop: click-and-drag to pan. Scroll wheel to zoom. The HUD is minimal — just a faint map in the corner showing position.

On mobile: touch-drag to pan. Pinch to zoom. The world is larger than the screen and that is the point.

## The 20+ Discovery Moments

The illustration contains at least 20 discoverable story beats, none of which are labelled or signposted:

1. **The Bua** — top left corner. She's already crying. Hover: *"She cried at the engagement. She cried at the mehendi booking. She will cry for three days after the wedding ends."*

2. **The Photographer** — she's shooting something. Click: her flash illuminates a hidden corner — a couple arguing near the flowers. Then dark again.

3. **The DJ (Sangeet zone)** — he's cuing up. Hover: the ambient music shifts — the zone's track rises slightly.

4. **The Biryani Counter** — a small figure with four plates. Hover: *"Day 3. This is not his first visit."*

5. **The Kids on the Roof** — three children. Click one: they scatter in three directions, each revealing a different detail of the scene they were near.

6. **The Father** — formal, stiff, checking his watch. Hover: *"He planned everything. He is the only one not enjoying it."*

7. **The Elephant** — in the background. Does it blink? Yes. Once. 8 seconds after you hover.

8. **The Mehndi Artist's Work** — zoom in close enough: the mehndi pattern on the bride's hand contains the TGIW logo embedded.

9. **Day 5 Zone** — visually darker than all others. Approach it: a whisper — *"You're not on the list for this one. Yet."*

10. **The Diya Flame** — the original cursor-following flame from Act 1 is still there, underneath everything. If you find it (click-hold on a specific dark corner), it pulses. An easter egg for those who remember.

11. **The Couple at Center** — looking at them for more than 3 seconds: they briefly turn to face the viewer. Then away. The only moment of direct eye contact in the experience.

12. **The Fireworks** — they're not always firing. Every 45 seconds, a new burst goes off. Different colour. Different corner. If you're not watching, you'll miss it.

... and eight more, each as specific and earned.

## Hidden Stories as Conversion Architecture

Each discovery moment serves a purpose:
- Moments 1–5: Extend dwell time
- Moments 6–10: Build emotional connection to characters
- Moments 11–15: Establish TGIW as a world, not a product
- Moments 16–20: Tease the five days without explaining them
- The Couple turning: the single moment that triggers the RSVP prompt

# SECTION 7: WORLD BUILDING

## Making Visitors Feel They Entered a Wedding

**Sensory completeness is the goal.**

A wedding is not visual. A wedding is audio. Smell. Temperature. The overwhelm of being surrounded by 200 people who all know each other except you.

Approximate this digitally:
- **Sound:** The first thing that makes someone feel present (see Section 12)
- **Visual density:** The illustration is the most valuable asset because it's *too much to see at once* — exactly like a real wedding
- **Discovery fatigue:** After 3 minutes, you've seen maybe 30% of the illustration. This is intentional. You're supposed to feel you've missed things.

## Easter Eggs

| Easter Egg | How to Find | Reward |
|---|---|---|
| The TGIW logo in the mehndi | Zoom to the bride's hand | Screen flash + *"You found it."* |
| The Diya (hidden) | Click-hold bottom-right darkness for 3s | All character whispers unlock simultaneously for 10s |
| The Couple's eye contact | Watch them for 3+ seconds | The RSVP prompt triggers — the *only* organic trigger |
| The DJ's track | Hover the DJ zone for 30s | A 15-second clip of an original TGIW theme plays |
| Day 5 | Discover all other 4 zones | Day 5 zone partially illuminates; a calendar date appears |
| The Hidden Character | Pan to the extreme right edge of the illustration | There is one character who is not part of the wedding — they're watching. The "founding guest." Clicking them unlocks the founding tier. |

## The Memory System

The site uses localStorage to remember:
- Which characters you've hovered
- Which zones you've discovered  
- Whether you've found any easter eggs

On return visits: *"Welcome back. You were here before."* The illustration starts from where you left the cursor. Characters you've already met have a faint gold outline — you know them now.

# SECTION 8: COPYWRITING DIRECTION

## Voice

**Not a brand talking. An invitation, whispering.**

The copy should feel handwritten in ink, not typed in a content management system. Every word should feel considered. Precious. As if someone deliberate chose it.

Reference voices: Arundhati Roy's precision. Vikram Seth's warmth. An old invitation card's formality.

## Tone Parameters

| Dimension | Level | In Practice |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery | 9/10 | Reveal nothing. Imply everything. |
| Humour | 4/10 | Specific, insider Indian wedding humour. Never broad. |
| Luxury | 8/10 | Restraint IS luxury. Use space. |
| Cultural depth | 10/10 | Write for someone who knows. Never explain. |
| Exclusivity | 7/10 | You are lucky to be here. This isn't false. |

## Headline Principles

**Rule 1: Never explain the product.** Headlines describe feelings, not features.

**Rule 2: Write in second person, present tense.** Not "you will feel" — "you feel."

**Rule 3: The Indian wedding insight.** Every headline should contain the specific emotional truth of an Indian wedding that only insiders know. Not the spectacle — the humanity.

Good headline examples:
- *"Some weddings you attend. This one, you enter."* ✓
- *"Five days. Sixty opinions. One family."*
- *"The only wedding where everyone cries for different reasons."*
- *"You didn't know everyone. By Day 3, you will."*
- *"Somewhere between the mehndi and the morning, something happened."*

**Rule 4: Bilingual moments are not translation. They are depth.** When Devanagari appears, it should carry something the English doesn't — or vice versa.

## CTA Principles

Never: "Sign Up." "Join Now." "Get Early Access."

Always: Frame as acceptance of an invitation.
- *"Accept your invitation"*
- *"Take your seat"*
- *"Join the guest list"*
- *"I'll be there"*

The CTA should make you feel like declining would be the strange choice.

# SECTION 9: WAITLIST MECHANICS

## The Core Mechanic: The Guest List

The waitlist is not a list. It is a **seating chart.**

Post-signup, users can see a visualized seating chart that shows (in silhouette, preserving privacy) where the other guests are seated. Their name occupies one seat. The chart fills over time. When it's full: *"The wedding is at capacity."*

This creates urgency without fakery. The chart is real. The seats are real.

## Tier Architecture: Three Roles, Not Three Prices

| Tier | Name | How You Get It | What It Means |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Guest | Sign up | You're invited. You're in. |
| 2 | VIP | Sign up early (first 500) OR refer 1 guest | You have a plus-one. Front section. Priority. |
| 3 | Family | Refer 3 guests | You're part of the family now. This is different. |

The mechanics:
- **Family tier** is never explained fully. VIPs get a hint: *"There's a tier above this. Bring your people."*
- **Founding Guests** (first 100): Their names appear in a special zone of the illustration. They are part of the art.

## The Referral System: The Family Tree

Referral is not a link-share mechanic. It is a **family tree.**

Post-signup, each user has a visual family tree. When they refer someone, a branch grows from their name. When that person refers someone, a branch grows from theirs. The tree is visible and beautiful. **The virality is the feature, not a footnote.**

Share mechanic: The shareable artifact is not a link. It is a personalized invitation addressed *from* the user *to* their contact. It arrives (via shareable image or email template) formatted as a formal wedding invitation card with the inviter's name in the sender line.

*"[Your name] requests the pleasure of your company."*

## Gamification Mechanics

| Action | Reward |
|---|---|
| Discover 5 characters | "You're starting to know people." — unlocks character glossary |
| Discover all 5 zones | Day 5 partially reveals |
| Refer 1 guest | Tier upgrade to VIP |
| Refer 3 guests | Family tier unlocked |
| Return visit | Character who says *"You again. Good."* |
| Find the founding guest easter egg | Founding Guest tier offered (limited) |

# SECTION 10: DESKTOP EXPERIENCE

## The Experience Philosophy

Desktop is the primary canvas. This is where the full world lives.

The desktop experience abandons the paradigm of "a page you scroll through" entirely. It becomes **a world you explore.**

## Navigation

**There is no navigation bar** during the exploration phase.

The only persistent UI elements:
- Top-left: TGIW wordmark (small, fades after 3s)
- Top-right: Current zone name (fades in as you enter a zone)
- Bottom-right: A small compass/map showing position in the world
- Bottom-left: Sound toggle (mute icon)

The RSVP doesn't live in navigation. It emerges from the world at the right moment.

## Scroll/Pan Behaviour

**Phase 1 (Pre-world):** Standard vertical scroll. Ceremonious. Slow. Each beat has breathing room.

**Phase 2 (World exploration):** The page physically reorients. The scroll axis shifts to horizontal + zoom. A transition animation (the illustration "unfolds" to full width) signals the mode change. This moment should feel like stepping through a door.

## Visual Hierarchy

1. Illustration (always dominant)
2. Light source (always visible, always active)
3. Character interactions (secondary — activated by proximity)
4. Type/UI (tertiary — never competes with the world)

## Conversion Placement

The RSVP does not have a fixed position on the page.

It appears when triggered (the couple's eye contact). It appears as an overlay *within* the world — a close-up of the invitation in the foreground, world soft-focused behind. Dismissing it returns you to exploration. The invitation waits in a sealed position at the bottom of the screen, available but never demanding.

# SECTION 11: MOBILE EXPERIENCE

## Do Not Adapt Desktop. Build Mobile-First.

Mobile is a different experience. Not a smaller version.

**The core mobile insight:** A phone is already intimate. It is held in your hands. It vibrates. It responds to touch. These are wedding feelings — intimacy, presence, physicality.

## The Mobile Paradigm: The Envelope

On mobile, the experience begins differently.

Not with the full illustration — but with a **single envelope**. Full-screen. Personal. As if it was delivered directly to this device.

The interaction model is gestural: **swipe to reveal, pinch to explore, tap to discover.**

The world on mobile is not a map you navigate. It is a series of **intimate scenes.** The illustration is revealed in full-bleed portrait vignettes — one character or zone at a time. Swipe right: another character. Swipe up: zoom in. The same illustration; a different way of experiencing it.

## Mobile-Specific Moments

- **Haptic feedback** on character discovery: a 30ms tap when you find something.
- **Tilt-based parallax:** The illustration responds to phone orientation. Tilt the phone: the depth layers shift. You feel the world has weight.
- **The envelope notification:** After signup, the phone vibrates once — a soft notification-weight pulse. *"Your invitation has been sealed."*

## Mobile Conversion Architecture

Mobile doesn't hide the RSVP. The form appears after three character discoveries (lower threshold than desktop). Mobile users need momentum maintained; the ask should come while they're invested but before they lose the thread.

# SECTION 12: AUDIO & ATMOSPHERE

## Yes. Sound Exists. But Not What You Think.

Not music. Not Bollywood. Not a "wedding song."

**Atmospheric sound design.** The kind that makes you forget it's there — until you turn it off and the world goes silent.

## The Sound Layers

**Layer 1 — Base atmosphere (always on, very low):**
- Room ambience of a large warm hall
- Distant crowd murmur (not intelligible)
- Wind through flowers and fabric

**Layer 2 — Positional audio (triggered by position in illustration):**
- Mehndi zone: soft dhol taps in the distance, women's conversation
- Sangeet zone: music building — a tabla pattern, not a full song
- Haldi zone: laughter, water sounds, shouting
- Wedding zone: shehnai, deep silence before the fire ceremony
- Day 5 zone: silence. Complete silence. A held breath.

**Layer 3 — Interaction audio:**
- Character hover: a soft chime, frequency tied to character's emotional state
- Envelope open: paper unfold sound, wax seal crack
- RSVP submit: distance firework burst, held for 2 seconds
- Tier upgrade: a single clear bell

## Implementation

Web Audio API with spatial positioning. Character sounds positioned in 2D space — closer characters are louder. Creates the sensation of actually being in the scene.

Sound off by default with a visible, one-tap enable. The first time sound turns on: make it worth it. A moment designed specifically for the sound-on state.

# SECTION 13: TECHNICAL DIRECTION

## Framework

**Next.js** — App Router, with React Three Fiber for the 3D/WebGL scene layer.

Rationale: The world exploration mode requires WebGL capability. React Three Fiber is mature enough for production use in 2026 and integrates cleanly with the rest of the stack.

## Animation Stack

**Primary (scroll/timeline):** GSAP 3 with ScrollTrigger. Non-negotiable — nothing else handles complex choreography at this precision.

**World/illustration:** PixiJS for the 2D illustration layer (characters, sprites, particle effects). Superior performance to canvas-only approaches for this level of interactivity.

**UI transitions:** Framer Motion for React component-level animations.

**Easing library:** Custom easing functions, not library defaults. The animations should be identifiable as TGIW-specific.

## Asset Strategy

**The illustration:** Must be prepared in layers — each character, each zone, the background architecture — as separate SVG/PNG elements. This is the biggest production task. Estimated: 40–60 hours of asset preparation.

**Compression:** All images through Squoosh pipeline. Next.js Image optimization. WebP with PNG fallback. The illustration at full quality will be 8–12MB; progressive loading covers the download time.

**Characters:** Individual PNGs at 2x resolution for retina. Sprite sheets for characters with animation frames.

## Performance Strategy

- **Critical path:** Black screen + diya flame loads in under 500ms (literally a CSS gradient and one SVG)
- **Progressive disclosure:** The invitation assets load while the user watches the opening. The full world loads while the user reads the invitation card.
- **Intersection Observer:** Characters only animate when in the visible viewport
- **Zone-based lazy loading:** The illustration is divided into 9 regions. Adjacent regions pre-load; non-adjacent regions stay lazy.

## Loading Strategy

The loading *is* the experience. The diya flame appearing in the dark IS the loading screen. If the page loads in 3 seconds, visitors wait in darkness watching a flame appear. This is not a bug. It is a feature.

Never show a progress bar. Never show a percentage. If loading takes longer than expected: the shehnai plays longer. The world just hasn't woken up yet.

# FINAL QUESTION: WHAT WINS AWWWARDS SITE OF THE YEAR

Sites that win Awwwards SOTY in the 2020s share one quality that separates them from sites that merely win SOTD:

**They create a memory you can't exactly describe.**

Not "the animations were smooth." Not "the design was beautiful." But something like: *"There was this moment when the couple turned and looked at me directly, and I felt like I'd been noticed by someone I wasn't supposed to notice me."*

That is the moment. Design for that.

The specific things that would make this Awwwards SOTY:

**1. The paradigm break.** The moment the world opens and scroll becomes pan — the experience stops behaving like a website. This is categorically different. Juries notice when an experience breaks its own rules in a way that serves the story.

**2. The illustration interactivity depth.** Most sites that use illustration use it decoratively. This treats an illustration as a *world.* The density of discoverable moments — 20+ character interactions — is the kind of thing that gets written about.

**3. The emotional specificity.** The whispers. *"Has been crying since the Welcome Dinner."* That line, in the context of the character it describes, at the moment of discovery, is funnier and more emotionally true than anything a generic wedding landing page would ever say. Cultural specificity at this level is rare in digital experiences.

**4. The referral mechanic as design object.** Most referral mechanics are afterthoughts. The family tree — a visual artefact that grows with each referral — is a piece of design that people will share a screenshot of, independent of the experience itself.

**5. The loading experience as experience.** The diya in darkness is the most underrated moment in this concept. When a jury member loads the page and encounters darkness and a single flame — before any content, before any explanation — they will sit up. That is the opening of a film, not a website.

**6. Day 5.** The most powerful tool in experience design is the thing you don't show. Day 5's description is a smudge of ink. The zone is the darkest corner of the illustration. It is completely, deliberately, withholding. The sites that win SOTY always have a secret that gets out.
